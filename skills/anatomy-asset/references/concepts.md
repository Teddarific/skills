# Concepts

How to get from a brief to an object worth drawing. An Anatomy figure maps a concept one-to-one onto a mechanism; an asset maps a *promise or a feeling* onto a thing. The mapping can be looser, but the thing must still be real enough to build.

## 1. Read the brief

Before ideas, write down five things, from the request or as sensible defaults you state:

- **What it's for.** Product, page and section: "the hero of a synth app's landing page", "the empty state of an inbox".
- **The one thing it says.** A promise ("make sound in seconds"), a feeling ("calm, focused"), or a fact ("works offline").
- **Who looks at it.** Developers expect instruments and machines; a consumer app may want warmth, home objects and soft materials.
- **Where it sits.** The page colour, the placement, the size it's shown at, the neighbours (a headline on the left, three spots in a row).
- **The brand.** Colours, and adjectives if the user gave any. A logo is rarely needed and never drawn as a flat stamp.

## 2. Three kinds of object

- **Literal.** The product as a beautifully made machine: a synth for a synth app, a camera for a photo tool. Strongest when the product *is* a thing or obviously could be.
- **Metaphor.** A real object whose working or character *is* the promise: a vault door for "never loses data", a sorting cabinet for "inbox triage", a kitchen timer for focus. Strongest for software, where the literal thing is a screen.
- **Scene.** A small diorama around one object: a workbench with a rocket on its launch rail and an open toolbox; a walnut tray with a timer, a candle and a tea tin. Strongest for heroes that need breadth, and for sets (each spot is one object from the same world).

Impossible combinations are allowed when they're built as if real: a lighthouse on a teacup, a server rack that is also a bookshelf, a mailbox with a tiny conveyor. The joke works only if every hinge and screw is right.

## 3. Generate, then choose

Write three to five one-line concepts that differ in kind, not just in object (one literal, one metaphor, one scene, one playful). For each, name the moment (section 6). Then pick with these tests:

- **Glance test.** Would someone name it in one second at the size it's shown?
- **Promise test.** Does the thing's working or character say the one thing, without a caption?
- **Detail test.** Does it have 50+ real parts? A smooth egg or a single cube fails.
- **Page test.** Does its silhouette fit the placement? A hero right wants something wider than tall or a low diagonal; a tall object (a lighthouse, a rocket) wants a hero centre or a narrow column.
- **Brand test.** Does its material and era match the mood? Machined aluminium for precision; oak, brass and felt for craft; rounded ABS plastic for friendly consumer tools.

Say which you'd pick and why, then build that one unless the user chooses another.

## 4. Mood and finish

Finish comes from proportions, part vocabulary and colour, not from texture:

| Mood | Proportions and parts | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Precise, technical | Thin slabs, fine bevels (0.4–0.6), rulers and ticks, knurls, many small screws | Greys, one sharp accent |
| Friendly, consumer | Big radii (r 4–8 on small parts), chunky buttons, rubber feet, fewer and bigger screws | Warm ground, a soft accent on the body |
| Craft, analogue | Wooden cheeks and trays, brass fittings, label plates with rule lines, hinges | Cream ground, a deep accent |
| Calm, focused | Few objects with air between, low heights, slow single motion | Low-contrast ground, muted accent |
| Energetic, launch | A diagonal composition, something lifted or mid-flight, stronger accent | Accent on the moving thing |

## 5. More mappings

| Brief | Object |
|---|---|
| Analytics dashboard | A brass surveyor's instrument on a tripod over a contour-map table |
| CI/CD pipeline | A small bottling line: conveyor, filler heads, capper, a crate at the end |
| Password manager | A wall of safe-deposit boxes, one drawer slid out, its key in the lock |
| Note-taking app | A writing desk with a pigeonhole hutch, a fountain pen and a stack of index cards |
| Video calls | A vintage broadcast camera on a pedestal, tally light lit |
| Recipes | A spice rack with labelled tins, one open, a brass scale beside it |
| Scheduling | A railway station clock and departure board with flip flaps |
| Feature flags | A row of rocker switches on a brass panel, one lit |
| Search | A library card catalogue, one drawer pulled, a card standing up |
| Backups | A tape library robot: shelves of cartridges and a picker on a rail |
| AI assistant | A tiny robot at a drafting table, or a typewriter whose keys type themselves |
| Fintech savings | A piggy bank as a precision instrument: coin slot, dial gauge, rivets |

## 6. The moment

An asset is one frame, so give it one moment the eye goes to: a knob cap lifted off its shaft, an envelope mid-flight into its pigeonhole, a drawer pulled out with its contents showing, a tally light on, a coin in the slot. Exactly one, carried by the accent or the `lit` tone, and placed at the composition's strong point (section 3 of `composition.md`). Ambient motion, if any, plays on this moment.

## 7. When the user already knows what they want

If the user names the object, the colours or the layout, that's the brief: skip the concept round and build it. If what they ask for is abstract ("floating cubes with our logo"), build it, but offer a real-object version alongside in one line, because that is where this style is strongest. Never refuse a style choice: lenient on taste, strict on craft.
